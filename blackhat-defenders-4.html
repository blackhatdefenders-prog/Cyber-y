// FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

A cyber attack is when someone uses technology to steal your data, spy on your activity, or damage your devices. Your bank accounts, personal photos, passwords, and identity can all be targeted. Staying informed dramatically reduces your risk.
Android phones benefit from security apps. Google Play Protect (built-in) provides basic protection. iPhones have stricter controls but still benefit from staying updated and avoiding suspicious links.
Warning signs: unexpected account activity, unfamiliar apps, battery draining fast, device running slow, strange pop-ups, friends receiving messages you didn't send, or unusual charges on your accounts.
2FA adds a second layer beyond just a password. When you log in, you also need a code sent to your phone or email. Even if someone steals your password, they can't access your account without the second step.
Public Wi-Fi is not secure. Hackers can intercept data on open networks. Avoid banking on public Wi-Fi. Use a VPN which encrypts your connection and keeps your activity private.
At least 12-16 characters with uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols. Avoid "password123". Use a password manager to generate and store unique passwords for every account.

Android Security Guide

Learn how to protect your Android phone from the most common threats in simple, easy steps.

⚠️
Did you know? Android powers 72% of smartphones — the #1 target for mobile malware. Over 3 million malicious apps are blocked by Google Play every year.
// Common Threats

Android Threats to Know

Understanding what attackers use helps you avoid them.

📱

Malware Apps

Malicious apps disguised as games or tools that steal data, track location, or send premium-rate SMS messages.

🎣

Phishing Apps

Fake banking or social media apps that look real but steal your login credentials and financial info.

👁️

Spyware

Hidden software monitoring your calls, messages, location, and camera without your knowledge.

📦

Fake APK Files

APK files from outside the Play Store claiming to be premium apps but containing malicious code.

📶

Public Wi-Fi Attacks

Hackers create fake hotspots or intercept traffic on open networks to steal passwords and data.

💬

SMS Phishing

Fake texts from "banks" or "delivery services" with malicious links to steal your information.

// Safety Guide

Android Security Best Practices

✓
Install Apps Only from Google Play StoreAvoid third-party app stores or downloading APK files from unknown websites.
✓
Enable Google Play ProtectPlay Store → Menu → Play Protect. Automatically scans apps for malware.
✓
Keep Android OS UpdatedSettings → System → Software Update. Updates contain critical security patches.
✓
Use Strong Screen Lock & BiometricsSet a 6-digit PIN and enable fingerprint or face recognition for secure access.
✓
Use a VPN on Public Wi-FiNever use banking apps on public Wi-Fi without a VPN to encrypt your connection.
✓
Review App PermissionsA flashlight asking for contacts is a red flag. Settings → Apps → Permissions.
✓
Enable Two-Factor AuthenticationAdd 2FA to Gmail, banking, and social media accounts for extra security.
✓
Avoid Suspicious LinksDon't tap links in SMS or WhatsApp from unknown senders — they lead to phishing sites.
✓
Avoid Unknown APK FilesEven from contacts — malware spreads through infected phones without the owner knowing.
✓
Enable Find My DeviceSettings → Security → Find My Device. Locate, lock, or wipe your phone remotely.

iPhone / iOS Security Guide

iPhones are secure by design, but no device is immune. Maximize your iPhone's security and privacy.

ℹ️
iOS Security Advantage: Apple's closed ecosystem and strict App Store policies make iOS harder to attack. However, phishing, iCloud attacks, and social engineering still affect millions of iPhone users.
// Known Threats

iPhone Threats to Watch

🎣

iCloud Phishing

Fake emails claiming your Apple ID is locked, tricking you into entering credentials on a fraudulent site.

🔗

Malicious Links

Dangerous links in Messages or Safari can exploit browser vulnerabilities or install rogue config profiles.

📲

Fake Config Profiles

Disguised as useful tools, these grant attackers deep access bypassing App Store restrictions.

🕵️

Stalkerware

Monitoring apps installed by someone with physical access that secretly track your activity.

📶

Wi-Fi Attacks

Connecting to malicious Wi-Fi exposes your traffic to man-in-the-middle attacks.

🧠

Social Engineering

Attackers impersonating Apple Support to trick you into giving up your Apple ID credentials.

// Protection Steps

iPhone Security Best Practices

✓
Enable Face ID or Strong PasscodeUse Face ID or Touch ID. Set a 6-digit PIN minimum — avoid birthdays or simple sequences.
✓
Keep iOS Always UpdatedSettings → General → Software Update → Automatic Updates. iOS updates fix critical vulnerabilities.
✓
Enable Two-Factor AuthenticationSettings → Your Name → Password & Security → Two-Factor Authentication.
✓
Review App Permissions RegularlySettings → Privacy & Security. Revoke unnecessary camera, mic, and location access.
✓
Strong Unique Apple ID PasswordApple will NEVER call you to ask for your password. Never share it with anyone.
✓
Avoid Suspicious Links & MessagesDon't tap links from unknown senders. Type website URLs directly into Safari.
✓
Enable Lockdown Mode (High Risk)Settings → Privacy & Security → Lockdown Mode for extreme protection against sophisticated attacks.
✓
Use iCloud KeychainLet iOS generate and store strong unique passwords for every site automatically.
✓
Enable Find My iPhoneSettings → Your Name → Find My. Remotely locate, lock, or erase your device if lost.
✓
Check for Unknown ProfilesSettings → General → VPN & Device Management. Remove unknown profiles immediately.

Laptop & Computer Security

Your computer holds your most sensitive data. Protect Windows and Mac from modern cyber threats.

// Threat Landscape

Computer Threats

🦠

Malware

Viruses, trojans, and worms that infect your computer, corrupt files, steal data, or give attackers remote control.

🔒

Ransomware

Encrypts all your files and demands payment to unlock them. Can destroy years of work in minutes.

📧

Email Phishing

Fake emails from "banks" or "Microsoft", tricking you into clicking malicious links or opening attachments.

👁️

Spyware / Keyloggers

Hidden programs recording every keystroke, capturing passwords and credit card numbers silently.

🌐

Drive-by Downloads

Malware that installs automatically when you visit a compromised website, without clicking anything.

🎭

Fake Software Updates

Pop-ups claiming your software is outdated — they install malware instead of real updates.

// Protection Guide

Computer Security Best Practices

✓
Install Trusted Antivirus SoftwareWindows Defender (free, built-in) is excellent. Mac has XProtect. Also consider Malwarebytes.
✓
Enable Your FirewallWindows: Settings → Windows Security → Firewall. Mac: System Settings → Network → Firewall.
✓
Keep OS & Software UpdatedEnable automatic updates. Most successful attacks exploit unpatched old vulnerabilities.
✓
Use a Password ManagerBitwarden or 1Password generate and store strong unique passwords — never reuse them.
✓
Backup Files Regularly3-2-1 rule: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite/cloud. Protects against ransomware.
✓
Avoid Unknown DownloadsOnly download software from official websites. Pirated software almost always contains malware.
✓
Lock Your Screen When AwayWindows: Win+L. Mac: Ctrl+Cmd+Q. Always lock when stepping away from your computer.
✓
Use HTTPS Websites OnlyLook for the padlock icon. Never enter passwords on sites showing "Not Secure".
✓
Be Wary of Email AttachmentsDon't open .exe, .zip, or .doc with macros from email — even from people you know.
✓
Use a Standard User AccountA standard account limits damage if malware infects your system — not an admin account daily.
// Email Safety

Spotting Phishing Emails

⚠
Check the Sender's Email Address"Amazon Support" can mask "amazon@sketchy-domain.ru". Always verify the actual email domain.
⚠
Urgency is a Red Flag"Act NOW or your account will be closed!" — Legitimate companies never create this kind of pressure.
⚠
Hover Before You ClickHover over links to see the real URL. If it doesn't match the claimed site, it's phishing.
⚠
Grammar & Spelling ErrorsMany phishing emails contain typos. Legitimate companies always proofread carefully.

Cyber Attack Awareness

Common cyber attacks explained in simple language. Knowledge is your first line of defense.

🎯
Key Fact: 95% of cybersecurity breaches are caused by human error. Understanding how attacks work means you can recognize and avoid them.
// Attack Types

Common Cyber Attacks Explained

🎣
PhishingVery Common

What it is: Attackers pretend to be a trusted company via email, text, or social media to trick you into revealing passwords, credit card numbers, or clicking malicious links.

✅ How to protect yourself: Verify the sender's email domain. Don't click links in unexpected emails — go directly to the website. When in doubt, call the company on their official number.
🦠
MalwareHigh Risk

What it is: "Malicious software" — any software designed to harm your device. Includes viruses, trojans (disguised as real software), worms (spread across networks), and adware.

✅ How to protect yourself: Keep antivirus active and updated. Only download from official sources. Avoid clicking ads claiming your device is infected — they install real malware.
🔒
RansomwareDevastating

What it is: Malware that encrypts all your files and demands a ransom payment in crypto to restore access. Victims include hospitals, schools, businesses, and individuals.

✅ How to protect yourself: Backup data regularly to external drive or cloud. Never pay the ransom. Keep software updated and avoid suspicious email attachments.
👁️
SpywareStealthy

What it is: Software that secretly monitors your activity — recording keystrokes, screenshots, camera/microphone access, location — and sends it to an attacker without you knowing.

✅ How to protect yourself: Run regular malware scans. Review app permissions. Watch for: overheating, slow device, unusual data usage. Factory reset if you suspect spyware.
🎭
Social EngineeringHuman-Based

What it is: Manipulating people psychologically to give up confidential information — impersonating tech support, creating fake urgency, or exploiting emotions like fear or greed.

✅ How to protect yourself: Be skeptical of unsolicited contact. Verify requests through a different channel. No legitimate company will ever ask for your password over phone or email.
// Red Flags

Universal Warning Signs

🚨
"Act Immediately or Lose Access"Artificial urgency is the #1 social engineering tactic. Legitimate services give you time to think.
🚨
Requests for Passwords or OTP CodesNo legitimate company — bank, Apple, Google, government — will ever ask for your password or OTP.
🚨
Too Good to Be True OffersPrize winnings, free iPhones, incredible investment returns — these are lures to steal from you.
🚨
Unknown Sender + Unexpected AttachmentEmail attachments from strangers are a primary malware delivery method. Never open them.
